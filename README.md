        <ul><li>Sugar</li>
        <li>Egg</li>

</ul>
<h2>Introduction</h2>
<ol>
    <li>Whisk the dry ingredients first. That’s flour, sugar, baking powder, and salt. Be mindful when measuring flour for this recipe. Too much flour makes pancakes dense. That’s why we recommend either weighing the flour or using the spoon-and-level method. To do it, fluff your flour in its container with a spoon. Spoon it into the measuring cup. Then, level it with a flat edge.

</li>
<li>Next, whisk your wet ingredients. In the second bowl (or measuring jug), whisk the wet ingredients (melted butter, egg, vanilla, and milk). If your milk is cold, the melted butter may solidify a little. Don’t worry about it. Those tiny pockets of butter will melt in the pan and help the pancakes puff up as they cook (similar to how cold butter helps homemade biscuits rise).

</li>
<li>Whisk the wet and dry ingredients until the streaks of flour disappear. Baking powder starts reacting the moment it hits the wet ingredients. This means air bubbles, and air bubbles mean fluffy pancakes. The more mixing you do, the more you pop those bubbles.

So, the more you fuss with the batter, the thinner your pancakes turn out. The same is true when making blueberry muffins and banana bread. You are looking for the batter consistency to be thick and pourable, like a heavy ribbon (see photos below). If it is too thick, add a little more milk.


</li>
<li>Preheat your skillet before adding your batter. This is the biggest mistake we see when beginners make pancakes. Preheat your skillet or griddle over medium for 1 to 2 minutes. To check if it is ready, sprinkle a few drops of water on the surface. They should “dance” and evaporate quickly. A bonus of this preheat time, is that it gives your flour a little time to hydrate, which means your first pancake will turn out just as well as your last.</li>
<li>Add butter, or try ghee (it’s better!). We used to add a pat of butter to the pan before pouring in the batter (see us do this in our video). Not anymore. We’ve converted to using ghee when cooking pancakes. Total game changer!</li>
<li>Flip pancakes when bubbles pop on the surface. This takes 1 to 2 minutes over medium heat, but trust your gut and adjust as you go. If they are browning too much, lower the heat to medium-low. If they are taking too long or turning out flabby, raise the heat to medium-high.</li>
<li>Keeping cooked pancakes warm. Place them on a wire rack set inside a baking sheet in a 200°F oven. We use this trick in our waffle recipe and French toast recipe, too. They stay warm, and the edges stay crispy until serving.</li>
